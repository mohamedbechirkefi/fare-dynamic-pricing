Does a moving price beat a well-tuned fixed one? In a simulation, it depends on scarcity.

FARE is a small, preregistered and reproducible simulation of a seller with 100 units, 60 periods and a deadline. A tuned fixed price competes with two simple dynamic rules under equal tuning budgets and identical simulated buyers; the protocols were frozen before any evaluation data existed, and 925,000 simulated seasons ran with none failing.

One defensible finding: at the base scenario the dynamic rules earn +3.47% and +3.94% more than the fixed price, but across 9 market cells 8 of 18 contrasts are negative, including every cell where capacity is loose. No general advantage of dynamic pricing is supported.

Caveat: everything is simulated, with myopic buyers and planning-choice parameters. It says nothing about any real seller, airline, hotel or fare.
